# Scene 05 research note — urban edge, vegetation and interchange context

**Research cutoff:** 2026-10-23  
**Build scope:** add locally plausible surroundings and regional transit context to the Secunderabad station study without treating nearby amenities as a surveyed layout.

## Evidence and confidence

### Metro and bus context

- L&T Metro Rail's official **Secunderabad East** and **Secunderabad West** station pages identify the two named Metro stations and list nearby railway, road, bus-stop and neighbourhood landmarks. The West page names Rathifile Bus Station among its nearby areas; the East page lists Rathifile Bus Station Road and local bus stops. These pages support representing the area's broader railway/Metro/bus relationship, but they do **not** give a surveyed Metro alignment, a measured station-to-terminal distance, an approved connection path, or a 3D station drawing.
- TGSRTC's bus-station list names **Jubilee Bus Station, Secunderabad** and **Rathifile–Secunderabad** as separate facilities. Rathifile is the station-near context used here; do not place the large JBS terminal beside the railway station. The roughly 2 km JBS separation found in secondary references is only a cautionary scale check, not a scene measurement.
- A 2019 *The Hindu* report says the Secunderabad East skywalk opened to Rathifile Bus Station but stopped short of directly reaching the railway station. This is an important caution: neighbourhood connectivity is not evidence of a single direct enclosed interchange or a particular physical bridge.
- A 2025 *The Hindu* report says HMDA invited consultants to prepare a conceptual design/DPR for an elevated pedestrian connection covering the railway station, Secunderabad East/West Metro stations, Rathifile, and nearby bus stops. This is a **proposal/design-stage** source, not an alignment drawing.
- A 24 July 2026 *Siasat* report, quoting an SCR official, says a skywalk is under construction to connect the railway station directly with the East/West Metro stations and a nearby bus stand. This newer report indicates work is underway, but provides no inspectable, surveyed route or section and does not establish commissioning. No skywalk geometry is drawn in either scene state; that omission is **not** a claim that construction is absent.
- A 2012 *The Hindu* report says the JBS–Falaknuma Metro corridor was diverted around the railway station, via Old Gandhi Hospital land, and would pass over the Bhoiguda Road Under Bridge; it also mentions another corridor near Oliphant Bridge. This supports a curved, grade-separated **Metro** context and the existence of a reported road-underbridge relationship, but it is not an as-built alignment/section drawing. It does **not** justify inventing a road crossing through the station yard or placing a specific ROB/RUB in the scene.
- The Institution of Civil Engineers' Hyderabad Metro project case study describes extensive precast/prestressed construction and a standard elevated-station structural system. Together with contemporary engineering reporting, this supports modeling a continuous concrete elevated guideway with regularly spaced piers and a restrained curve. It does not establish the Secunderabad segment's exact radius, pier spacing, profile, or alignment.
- South Central Railway's 2024 redevelopment release describes planned Metro and bus connectivity as project intent. It does not verify that every connection is built, commissioned, or located where shown in a schematic.

### Road access and current redevelopment

- SCR advisories and dated 2025–26 reporting describe changing passenger gates, drop-off, parking and traffic circulation during redevelopment, including a Platform 10-side basement facility and temporary Platform 1-side parking restrictions. These support depicting legible station-front circulation as a design goal, but they are time-specific and do not provide a surveyed road plan. The outer streets and access branches in this prototype therefore remain illustrative.
- No inspectable, authoritative station-area road/yard general-arrangement drawing was found for this pass. No new ROB/RUB or road-over-track alignment is asserted; road continuity is improved around the outside of the schematic station envelope instead.

### Urban vegetation

- The Hyderabad Annual Tree Biodiversity Survey 2024, reported by *The Hindu* and published by WWF-India Hyderabad collaborators, documents 120 tree species and identifies Copperpod (*Peltophorum pterocarpum*), Neem (*Azadirachta indica*) and Pongamia (*Millettia pinnata*) among the most frequently recorded species. These species form the principal palette for the scene's procedural tree silhouettes; their modeled abundance and locations are not an inventory of Secunderabad station.
- A GHMC-reported KBR Park tree list includes Banyan and Royal Palm among locally recorded park species. The scene includes occasional Banyan-like and palm-like forms as additional broad urban/park cues. A park inventory does not establish their presence or frequency at the railway boundary.
- Low scrub, dry grass and stones are generic Deccan edge dressing. No station-side vegetation survey or georeferenced planting plan was available.

## What is modeled

- The streetscape has varied warm, neutral and cool facade palettes, irregular block offsets/heights, rooftop water tanks, occasional solar panels, cellular masts, satellite dishes, balconies, shop awnings and small unbranded boards. These are generic urban massing/detail cues, not traced buildings or parcels.
- Asphalt and footpaths have deterministic procedural aggregate/paver textures. The station-edge loop now continues as generic side streets into the urban horizon and has a short Rathifile-context driveway, extended sidewalks/kerbs, patched asphalt, storm grates and manhole covers. Segmented boundary walls, access gaps, streetlights, dry scrub, stones and grass tufts remain illustrative; none is an as-built railway boundary, public-road alignment, property line or landscape plan.
- The Metro context is a long, gently curved continuous concrete guideway with repeated single-column piers/cross-heads, twin trackway ribbons, a generic two-side-platform station, lift cores and stair access. The curve and structure are designed massing cues, not a surveyed alignment, radius, profile or pier schedule.
- The Rathifile context is a separately modeled, open three-canopy bus-terminal massing with six unbranded static buses, two small motorcycles, a kiosk, benches, bins and static low-poly passenger silhouettes. Its footprint, bay count and exact placement are not measured; it is **not** Jubilee Bus Station/JBS. Station-front and Metro/bus passengers are anonymous static scale figures.
- Twelve low-poly cars/auto-rickshaws move slowly along the outer schematic access roads. Ambient motion is throttled to roughly 10–12 renders per second, pauses with a hidden tab, and respects `prefers-reduced-motion`; it is not a traffic count, schedule, route or hardware-performance guarantee. Forecourt vehicles and buses remain static.
- The Metro has its own schematic street-to-platform access cores and stairs. No direct Metro–railway/bus skywalk, common concourse, shared platform or exact pedestrian-transfer path is modeled. Recent SCR reporting says a Metro skywalk is under construction, but no inspectable route was available; this omission avoids inventing an alignment and is not a claim that construction is absent. The props remain a legible context vignette outside the schematic track group. No Metro track gauge, signaling, power system, operating timetable or operator-specific rolling stock is asserted.
- The trees, moving traffic and Metro/bus props are placed with the same conceptual surroundings group. Scene metadata and labels call out massing rather than a location plan. “Rathifile Bus Station” is a contextual label supported by operator-area listings, not a measured sign design or surveyed entrance.

## Lighting and limits

- The afternoon key light is deliberately warmer and moved to illuminate the station from the foreground-side azimuth; one static directional shadow map is slightly higher resolution and less soft. A longer linear fog falloff adds restrained distance haze. No SSAO/HBAO pass or additional real-time shadow lights were added; those costs should be evaluated on target hardware before adoption. Sun direction, intensity, color, fog and shadow settings are an artistic real-time study, not local meteorology, calibrated photography, a photometric design or a hardware performance benchmark. Night lighting remains a separate qualitative study.
- All new geometry is procedural and locally authored. Nothing in this pass adds a verified station-yard change, a specific Metro alignment, a bus interchange circulation plan, an architectural survey, or a road network reconstruction. Exact locations, scales, species, quantities and connections remain assumptions until supported by primary drawings or georeferenced field data.

## Validation for this pass

- `npm run build`, `GITHUB_PAGES=true npm run build`, and `git diff --check` pass. Vite reports the expected large-chunk advisory; the new build is about 730 kB (193 kB gzip).
- Headless Chromium 153 with SwiftShader rendered the scene at 1,280 × 800 with WebGL2 active. Current/planned states and the exterior camera control were exercised with no browser errors, failed requests or bad responses. The loader now clears after the first actual scene render rather than after a fixed delay.
- This software-WebGL check validates loading and interaction only; it is not an FPS or hardware-GPU performance guarantee. Ambient road traffic is limited to roughly 10–12 scheduled renders per second and respects the reduced-motion preference.
